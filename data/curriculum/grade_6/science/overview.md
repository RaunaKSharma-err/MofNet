# Grade 6 Science — Nepal Curriculum

## Living Things and Their Environment

All living things need food, water, air, and shelter to survive. Plants make their own food through photosynthesis using sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water. Animals depend on plants or other animals for food.

The environment includes both living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) components. Biotic components include plants, animals, fungi, and microorganisms. Abiotic components include soil, water, air, sunlight, and temperature.

## Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants prepare food. Chlorophyll in leaves traps sunlight energy. The plant absorbs carbon dioxide from the air and water from the soil. It produces glucose (food) and releases oxygen as a byproduct.

The simplified equation is: Carbon dioxide + Water + Sunlight → Glucose + Oxygen.

## Food Chain

A food chain shows how energy passes from one organism to another. Producers (plants) are eaten by primary consumers (herbivores). Primary consumers are eaten by secondary consumers (carnivores). Decomposers break down dead organisms and return nutrients to the soil.

Example: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle.

## Human Body Systems

The digestive system breaks down food into nutrients. The respiratory system helps us breathe in oxygen and remove carbon dioxide. The circulatory system transports blood, nutrients, and oxygen throughout the body.

## Health and Hygiene

Clean drinking water, balanced diet, regular exercise, and proper sanitation prevent many diseases common in rural communities. Washing hands before eating and after using the toilet reduces the spread of germs.
